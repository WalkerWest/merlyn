Three engagement modes

Three ways to deploy the L3 layer

The L3 capability is the same in all three modes — agents act on your data, twins simulate scenarios, supervisors stay in control. What changes is who builds it and who owns the operational muscle. L3's DIY path requires the Agent Construction track at L1 — the most technical track in the curriculum.

Mode A · DIY

You build it

After your team finishes the L1 Agent Construction track, they configure twins and agents on the Regularis platform themselves. Industry templates accelerate the work. Regularis provides architecture advisory and the platform; your team owns the build and operates the layer.

  • Best for: orgs with ML/agent team + multi-year horizon
  • L1 prerequisite: Agent Construction track
  • Regularis role: platform + advisory
  • Time to first deployment: 1–6 months (after training)
Mode B · Done-for-you

We build it

Regularis builds bespoke agents and twins around your specific data, decision points, and operational scenarios. For twins, quarterly executive facilitation is included; for operational agents, ongoing tuning and supervision are included. Your team uses the output; we keep it current. Most L3 launches start here.

  • Best for: orgs wanting agentic AI without ML team overhead
  • L1 prerequisite: Business Leaders track (or relevant supervisor track)
  • Regularis role: bespoke build + ongoing operation
  • Time to first deployment: 6–12 months
Mode C · Collaborative

We build it together

Regularis architects code alongside your engineering team. Knowledge transfer is scoped into the engagement — your team graduates to operating the agents and twins themselves. Scoped so your team can take over as Mode A (DIY) once its capability has caught up.

  • Best for: orgs building internal capability + needing decision-grade twins now
  • L1 prerequisite: Agent Construction track in parallel
  • Regularis role: joint build + capability transfer
  • Time to first deployment: 4–8 months

Engagement patterns

What an L3 engagement looks like

Continuous operation, cadenced human checkpoints

L3 engagements typically combine continuous operation with human oversight. Agents run their assigned tasks day-to-day, supervised by your team through TeamIQ. Twin engagements add scheduled scenario sessions — typically quarterly for executive decision twins — where leadership runs pre-modeled scenarios and the twin responds in real time. Common touchpoints:

Capital allocation (twin)

Run scenarios against your financial twin before committing the next dollar of investment.

Operational agents (agent)

Routing, scheduling, and document-processing agents handle tasks day-to-day. Exceptions surface for your team.

Market scenario planning (twin)

Model demand shocks, supplier failures, or capacity changes against your operational data.

Workflow automation (agent)

Internal workflows — approvals, intake, follow-up — run on agents tuned for your processes.

Regional manufacturer · capacity planning

A regional manufacturer wants to model how production capacity scales as they add a second shift, commission new equipment, or expand into a second facility. They configure their twin from Regularis's manufacturing template, plug in their ERP and production data, and run scenarios. Their CFO and COO use it monthly to align on capacity investment decisions. Their team has been through the Agent Construction track at L1, so they own day-to-day operation.

Regional logistics company · dispatch + load planning agents

A regional logistics company wants to automate load planning and dispatch decisions across their fleet — optimizing for fuel cost, driver time, delivery SLAs, and equipment utilization. Regularis builds agents integrated with their TMS and ELD systems that propose load assignments throughout the day; dispatchers approve or override through TeamIQ. Over six months the agents move from suggesting all decisions to handling the routine 80% autonomously with human review of exceptions. Operations team supervises continuously; weekly ops reviews catch edge cases.

What it costs

Pricing varies by engagement mode. Mode A (DIY) is the platform subscription — your trained team configures twins from templates and operates them. Mode B (Done-for-you) is Regularis building bespoke twins for you. Mode C (Collaborative) is a joint build with scoped knowledge transfer.

The same Regularis AI OS underlies all three modes — twins hosted on Dominion infrastructure, same simulation engine, same scenario framework. What changes is who built the specific twin and who operates it day-to-day. Migration between modes is a configuration change, not a re-platforming.

Two SLAs, one relationship. Regularis's L3 subscription (any mode) includes the application SLA — your twin's data ingestion, simulation accuracy, scenario engine, and (where applicable) executive facilitation are running and current. The infrastructure underneath (GPUs, Kubernetes, storage, network, monitoring) runs on Dominion Hyperscale with its own platform-layer SLA. The two SLAs stack rather than overlap.
