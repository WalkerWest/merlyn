Regularis Desk

Some work still needs
a person.

Regularis Desk is the channel between your business processes and the people who run them. When a workflow reaches a step that software can't finish on its own — an application with no API, a judgment call, an approval — Desk brings that step to the right person and carries the result back.

For software you use in a browser it is an add-on; for software installed on the desktop, a small tray app. Your organization provisions a workspace and issues you an account. It is not a chatbot and not a monitoring tool.

How it works

Desk is one end of a governed workflow. The other end is Ordinarium, the discipline that decides what gets routed, to whom, and what happens to the answer.

A process reaches a human step

A workflow needs something only a person can do — a decision, an approval, or an action in a system with no API.

Desk brings it to the right person

The task appears in the tray or the browser, with the context needed to act on it. No inbox to watch, no queue to remember to check.

The result goes back, on the record

The outcome returns to the process and the workflow continues. Who did what, and when, is recorded as part of the normal run — not as extra instrumentation.

It works in both directions

Desk is a channel, not a mailbox.

The business reaches the operator

A running process hands a step to a named person and waits for the answer. This is most of the traffic: approvals, exceptions, judgment calls, and work inside applications that were never built to be automated.

The operator starts the work

A person can also initiate a process from Desk — kicking off a workflow from the desktop or from the page they are already looking at, without switching to another system to do it.

The first application: taking a card payment beside software that can’t

A front desk whose software will never add payments runs a card terminal next to the keyboard and types every amount twice — once into the terminal, once into the ledger. Desk reads the amount off the screen, hands it to the terminal, and posts the result back where it belongs. The software vendor is never asked for anything.

Notice, or be asked

Desk sees a balance committed in the application and offers to collect it — or the operator summons it with a hotkey, or a toolbar button in a browser-based application.

Read the amount off the screen

On a profiled application the balance and the ledger reference arrive filled and highlighted. On one Desk has never seen, the field waits for the operator to type — and the product still works.

Charge it on the terminal, not the PC

The device at the counter wakes and prompts; the customer dips or taps. Only the approval comes back — auth code, last four, a token. Card data never touches the workstation, the browser, or the application.

Post the result back, on the record

Desk drives the application’s own payment entry and the operator watches it happen — or, where it can’t, shows the exact line it will type and waits for the cursor to land in the right field. Nothing is typed anywhere the operator did not sanction.

Two details that turned out to matter more than they sound.

The manual version is already the product. Every capability ships in tiers, cheapest first: type the amount and paste the result; confirm a preview and let Desk type it; or balance pre-filled and payment auto-posted. A front desk on software nobody has profiled takes its first payment within the hour at the first tier, and graduates when its profile is recorded — same workflow, fewer keystrokes.

And posting state is explicit, never assumed. Every transaction carries whether the ledger actually received it — auto-posted, typed and confirmed, copied but unverified, or not posted — so end-of-day reconciliation can tell “the ledger has it” from “we assume the ledger has it.” If the charge succeeds and the posting fails, the operator sees exactly that, with the result one paste away.

Built, and under test. Charges reach a real terminal at the counter; posting is verified against two real ledgers at the highest tier they permit. No front desk is running it yet.
